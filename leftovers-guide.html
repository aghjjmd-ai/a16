The Leftovers Guide

Leftovers are not second-best food. Handled properly they are a head start on tomorrow’s dinner. Handled carelessly they are a waste, or worse.

Cool it quickly

Bacteria multiply fastest in warm food, so the aim is to get leftovers from hot to cold as quickly as you reasonably can, and into the fridge within two hours of cooking.

  • Divide large amounts into shallow containers. A deep pot of stew can stay warm in the middle for hours.
  • Leave lids ajar until the steam has gone, then seal.
  • Do not put a large, very hot pan straight into the fridge; it warms everything around it. Portion it first.
  • Rice needs particular care. Spread it on a tray to cool within an hour, then refrigerate.

How long things keep

FoodIn the fridgeIn the freezer
Cooked meat and poultry2–3 days2–3 months
Stews, curries, chilli2–3 days3 months
Cooked rice1 day1 month
Cooked pasta2 daysNot ideal
Roast vegetables3 days3 months
Cooked fish1–2 days1 month
Soup3 days3 months
If you are not sure, do not eat it. Smell is not a reliable guide; many of the bacteria that cause illness do not change how food looks or smells.

Reheat properly

Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot all the way through, not merely warm. Stir halfway, especially in a microwave, which heats unevenly. Reheat only the portion you intend to eat and only once. Repeated warming and cooling is where the risk lies.

Make it a different meal

Leftovers disappoint when they are served as the same dinner, slightly worse. Change the form.

  • Roast chicken becomes a noodle soup, a pie filling or a salad with a sharp dressing.
  • Roast vegetables become a frittata, a pasta sauce when blended with stock, or a filling for wraps.
  • Cooked rice becomes fried rice with egg and whatever vegetables are to hand.
  • Mashed potato becomes fish cakes, potato cakes or a topping for a pie.
  • Chilli or bolognese becomes a baked potato filling, stuffed peppers or a quick baked pasta.

The Leftover Remix Finder suggests three ideas for whatever you have.

Add something fresh

Reheated food loses brightness. Put it back at the table with a squeeze of lemon, a handful of chopped herbs, a spoon of yogurt or something crunchy on top. It takes thirty seconds and changes the whole plate.

Plan them on purpose

The best leftovers are intended. When you shop, decide which dinner will feed a second one, and cook the extra deliberately: a larger chicken, a double batch of sauce, an extra tray of vegetables. Then tomorrow is half done before you start.

Use the freezer

If you will not eat something within two days, freeze it on the day it was cooked, in portions, flat in labelled bags. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat until piping hot. A freezer drawer of single portions is the most useful thing a busy cook can own.
